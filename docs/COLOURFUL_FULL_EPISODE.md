The owner requested a full video after the 12-second study proved insufficient. `cartoon_explainer_v3` is an additive full-episode route in the existing worker, not a replacement business system. The example targets **44 seconds**, 1080×1920, native/encoded 24fps, Indonesian local speech, grouped burned-in captions and the unchanged original EGON logo. The earlier 36-second dark pilot remains visually rejected; the short study remains an archive, not a production prerequisite.

## Story and pacing

Seven shots progress through one beginner-friendly journey: fruit (3s), seed reveal (5s), processing/drying (6s), roasting (7s), grinding (7s), one pour-over illustration (8s), then a practical buying takeaway and curiosity CTA (8s). Explicit shot times must all be positive/finite and sum to the episode target. Narration is measured with the local Indonesian voice. If it will not fit a shot, the worker stops for a rewrite before 3D rendering; it never silently cuts or speeds speech to force a fit.

The practical action is to mention the intended brewer when buying/requesting ground coffee. Scenes show colour change, a schematic moving burr, grounds becoming smaller, water meeting the coffee bed, grounds retained in the dripper, and liquid collecting in an open mug. This is educational cartoon art, not an EGON physical experiment, equipment specification or real-time recipe. No universal processing order, precise brewing ratio, temperature, time, roast level, product taste or universal two-seed count is claimed.

## Reuse and disk limits

The portable example renders all shots from original procedural assets. A local job may explicitly reference a registered `generated_animation` opening cache: verify its SHA-256, 1080×1920 dimensions, native 24fps, frame count and exactly matching first two scene timings (3+5 seconds). A modified or unknown cache fails closed. Reuse covers only the first 192 native frames; the subsequent 864 frames have new scenes. The opening is neither stretched nor looped.

The Blender route streams one transient PNG at a time to FFmpeg and retains twelve actual rendered/verified frame samples for motion checks. The encoded native-frame count, entire MP4 decode, actual sampled pixel motion, voice signal, captions and canonical logo are checked. Keep the same production ID and cumulative cost history across revisions/retries. This route currently requires native and encoded 24fps. Technical QA is not human aesthetic or publishing approval.
